Why You Should Read

Thu Oct 29 2026 19:00:00 GMT-0500 (Central Daylight Time)

Previously, I've written about how to read better, and how to approach difficult books.

However, I think one of the more important questions is why you should even read to begin with? After all, it isn't the 1800's anymore, there are a lot of more formats out there to learn from. There is TV, lectures, podcasts, videos, etc.

Admittedly, a lot of these formats have done amazing jobs at conveying information on a variety of different topics. I'm not dismissing any of these formats at "bad" or 'low-brow' in anyway at all.

Actually, I argue that some of these formats are better at conveying somethings than just raw words on paper. However, many still read and for good reason. It's for the simple fact that sometimes reading is simply the only way.

You only know what you know

It is no surprise that one person can only know they already know. You cannot be aware of what you aren't aware of.

If you're somebody that experiences a 'low-effort imagination' or a shortage of ideas in someway, you should absolutely read, especially fiction of any kind.

Books introduce you to many ideas, concepts, ways of telling scenes, a characters thoughts, perspectives, etc. A good book can widen your horizon of what you are able to imagine, and this can in-turn change how you view the world and perceive the universe that you're in.

This is especially important if you wish to become a decent writer, or somebody that is well-spoken, and is able to convey their thoughts in such a smooth and clear manner.

If you feel that you say a lot of things which often sound better in your head, than when spoken out loud, reading can greatly aid in that. In george orwell's 1984, one of the methods used to keep the population dumb and ignorant was to prevent them from having a good lexicon.

Instead of things being "terrible", "horrific", "awful", etc, they were just "bad" or "good". This prevents us from understanding one another and being able to convey complex sentences and build ideas off one another.

In a similar way, this can prevent us from being effective in how we speak, especially when discussing complex ideas. In the most extreme way, it can even prevent us from being able to learn new things.

The primary mechanism for learning is pattern recognition by cross-referencing ideas together to make sense of what we are learning. If we don't already have concepts to reference from, we can be as the saying goes: "As dumb as a box of rocks."
